# Projected mixed FDM

Build with `build.bat` from `alice2/`, then run `build/bin/Release/alice2.exe --check-projected-mixed-fdm`. The active sketch is `userSrc/geometry/sketch_projected_mixed_fdm.cpp` (`#define __MAIN__`). It reads `data/tna.obj`, welds duplicate face-corner vertices, selects the same yellow cable edges as `sketch_bestfit_tna_v2.cpp`, and places interior cable nodes below the mesh with vertical tension connectors. The cable-offset slider rebuilds this graph. Because this OBJ has no black support markers, its six boundary yellow vertices are fixed in XYZ for the signed FDM solve.

`ProjectedMixedFDM.h` accepts arbitrary graph edges, constant nodal loads, and fixed coordinate masks. Positive density means compression; negative means tension. The equilibrium operator internally uses `-q` so that the physical geometry and loads keep the same meaning. The solver first reports a fixed-input-geometry residual, then alternates reference projection, bounded density correction, and full signed SparseLU recovery. Every accepted state is checked against the actual equilibrium residual. A zero-load solve anchors the first edge's density to its declared target magnitude.

`supportHorizontalWeight` enables a second balancing stage after the shape fit. It differentiates the actual recovered support reactions with respect to edge densities, increases tension densities in magnitude, and permits compression densities to move by at most 10% of their fitted values. Each trial is recovered and checked for equilibrium. The slider also sets a shape-error allowance of `1 + 0.2 * weight` times the shape-only error. The sketch reports the remaining horizontal support-force norm and shows `support_shape_limit` when this allowance prevents further reduction. On the source `tna.obj`, weight 10 reduced that norm from 12.95 to 10.07; driving it close to zero required a much larger shape change. The residual is therefore shown rather than treating an equilibrium residual at free nodes as proof of zero support force.

The sketch uses the same starting load parameters as `sketch_bestfit_tna_v2.cpp`: 0.001 nodal load on surface vertices plus tributary-area weight with density 1. The areas are frozen at the input mesh, so this is a constant-load approximation. It does not derive loads from a chosen density field. A small fixed-geometry residual indicates whether the input is close to a sign-feasible equilibrium; a near-zero final force residual alone does not imply the recovered shape is close to the input.

The current implementation handles constant nodal loads only. It does not test material buckling, detect every nearly singular but solvable matrix, or claim a speedup over the existing solver. A `stagnated` status means no further admissible shape-improving step was found.
